## I. Executive Synthesis: Why the Apartment 1B Kitchen Was Built, Why It Is Harassment, and Why It Establishes Criminal Mens Rea

### 1. Why They Built the Kitchen in Apartment 1B (The Litigation & Physical End-Run)
1. **The Failed Eviction Campaign (2016–2022):**
   - Since at least 2003, Chelsea Partners I, LLC rented Apartment 1A (and the adjacent first-floor units) exclusively as residential apartments using residential lease forms ("to be used only as a private Apartment to live in"). Marcelo Romano moved into Apartment 1A on June 22, 2010 under a residential lease ($1,550/month) renewed through 2015 ($2,500/month).
   - In 2016, Chelsea Partners sued Mr. Romano in a **residential nonpayment proceeding** (Index No. 055816/16), swearing that the apartment was rented for dwelling purposes and claiming it was exempt from rent stabilization due to **high-rent vacancy deregulation**.
   - In November 2016, Mr. Romano discovered that the building lacked a residential Certificate of Occupancy (C of O) for the first floor (triggering a rent bar under Multiple Dwelling Law §§ 301–302) and obtained DHCR records showing the apartment was rent-stabilized (supported by J-51 tax benefits running through 2011/2012, overlapping his 2010 move-in).
   - After discontinuing the 2016 nonpayment case, Chelsea Partners commenced the holdover proceeding (*Chelsea Partners I, LLC v. Marcelo Romano*, Index No. LT-081870-17/NY) in December 2017, pivoting to the theory that Mr. Romano breached a substantial obligation of his tenancy by living residentially in a space designated on the 1977 C of O as commercial/art studio ("no living"), and claiming deregulation via a 1975–1977 "substantial rehabilitation."
   - Under settled New York law (*625 West End, Inc. v. Howard*; *Zaccaro v. Freidenbergs*), a landlord who creates an illegal residential tenancy cannot evict a tenant unless the landlord proves that it **exhausted all administrative remedies** to legalize the residential use or that legalization is **impossible / unduly burdensome**.
   - Both Judge Frances A. Ortiz (January 21, 2020 Order, NYSCEF Doc. 10) and Judge Karen May Bacdayan (November 8/10, 2022 Amended Decision/Order, NYSCEF Doc. 70/71) rejected Chelsea Partners' summary judgment motions. Judge Bacdayan granted summary judgment to Mr. Romano dismissing the holdover petition, holding that:
     - Chelsea Partners itself created the illegality ("A wrongdoer should not be able to take refuge behind the shield of his own wrong");
     - Chelsea Partners mischaracterized the January 25, 2019 DOB Notice of Objections (which cited only a zoning objection under ZR § 32-422, listed "None" under Egress, and stated "Plan review will be continuing after resolving the zoning objections"); and
     - Architect Jeff Akerman's letter and affidavit (NYSCEF Docs. 26, 68) claiming "unresolvable" zoning and egress obstacles were conclusory and non-probative.

2. **Manufacturing Physical "Impossibility" and Executing Self-Help Constructive Eviction:**
   - As Mr. Romano warned his counsel in real time in October 2019, May 2022, and June 2022 (`hcourt/email` records), while the litigation was pending and immediately after it failed, the landlord began altering the adjacent spaces ("The restaurant that just opened here is now taking over the barber...    - Specifically, under **NYC Zoning Resolution § 32-422** (the exact statute cited in the 2019 DOB objection sheet), commercial uses in C6 districts cannot be located on the same story as, or above, residential dwelling units if converting to residential. By converting the adjacent first-floor residential apartment (**Apartment 1B**) into an unpermitted commercial kitchen / dumpling manufacturing facility for **Hey Yuet**, the landlord and operators sought to:
     1. **Physically entrench commercial/manufacturing use on the first floor** to manufacture a fait accompli against residential legalization of the first floor; and
